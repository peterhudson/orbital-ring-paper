# Active Support Work Breakdown, Version 1

## Objective
Retire the highest-risk unknowns in a staged way until an active-support demonstrator becomes credible.

## Stage 0: Define the target
Deliverables:
- What exactly is the demonstrator supposed to prove?
- What height, silhouette, occupancy, wind survival, and cost envelope define success?
- What event or venue class is realistic?

## Stage 1: Single-member physics
Goal:
Show that one coaxial active-support member can generate stable controllable force.

Tasks:
- Derive support-force model from rotor speed, rotor mass per length, curvature or deflection, and magnetic coupling architecture
- Estimate achievable force per meter
- Estimate power required per meter
- Bound stored kinetic energy and failure consequences
- Identify candidate materials and magnetic architectures

## Stage 2: Single-member control
Goal:
Show that one active member can be stabilized under disturbance.

Tasks:
- Build a dynamic model of rotor plus stator coupling
- Add sensor and actuator limits
- Simulate disturbances and control delay
- Quantify stability margins
- Identify non-negotiable sensing requirements

## Stage 3: Multi-member bundle
Goal:
Understand whether bundles or buses of active members cooperate or fight each other.

Tasks:
- Model coupled oscillation modes
- Study helical geometry options
- Study load sharing and control allocation
- Determine whether bundle topology helps or worsens fault tolerance

## Stage 4: Envelope structure
Goal:
Determine whether a large Kevlar or tension-shell envelope is an asset or a nightmare.

Tasks:
- Model inflated shell modes
- Study wind coupling and aeroelastic effects
- Determine whether the envelope should carry meaningful load or only shaping load
- Compare fabric, cable-net, and hybrid shells

## Stage 5: Failure engineering
Goal:
Convince serious people that the system can fail without becoming an urban myth.

Tasks:
- Rotor rupture scenarios
- Levitation loss scenarios
- Power outage scenarios
- Actuator runaway scenarios
- Progressive collapse scenarios
- Emergency spin-down / isolation methods

## Stage 6: Scaled demonstrators
Goal:
Build the ladder.

Suggested sequence:
- tabletop member demonstrator
- room-scale actively supported beam or arch
- outdoor tower demonstrator at tens of meters
- landmark-scale public installation
- kilometer-scale structure
- two-kilometer flagship demonstrator

## Stage 7: Story and legitimacy
Goal:
Translate engineering into funding and permission.

Tasks:
- Produce visualizations that explain the structural principle clearly
- Define public-safe operating modes
- Identify world events, host cities, insurers, and regulators
- Frame the demonstrator as the birth of a new structural discipline

## Recommendation
Start with Stage 1 and Stage 2 as the immediate analytical program.
If those fail, the rest is theatre.
If those survive, we earn the right to think bigger.
