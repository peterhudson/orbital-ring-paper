- Mirrored helical paired lanes can produce clean axial/tangential tug channels with first-order circumferential cancellation. Opposed azimuthal sectors can turn those tugs into bending moments for local shape control.

- Important new control conclusion: discrete slug section occupancy does not require literal slug insertion/removal. It can change kinematically through spacing expansion/compression. The cleanest current architecture is distributed section-wide speed control without fill/drain hardware at ordinary tug stations, with optional storage/fill-drain only at special nodes.
- Boundary-only upward speed steps into an already occupied section look physically bad because accelerated entrants can catch slower incumbents ahead. Boundary-only slowdown is less pathological, but still constrained by minimum safe headway.
- Distributed-transition control is now the preferred ordinary architecture. New key results: spacing evolves approximately as s_dot ≈ (∂v/∂x) s; safe monotone slowdown is governed by h v_1 ≥ ℓ_s + g_min; upward ramps require actuation delay τ_d < [h v_0 - (ℓ_s + g_min)] / (v_1 - v_0); and smooth distributed transitions preserve integrated tug authority with q_z(x) = -2 N_s m_dot cos(α) dv/dx and ΔT = 2 N_s m_dot Δv cos(α).
- Distributed transitions seem sufficient for low-bandwidth ring-scale load transport and shape control. Hard tug nodes still look useful mainly at special interfaces such as tether nodes, startup/shutdown hubs, or fault-handling points.
- Added a helix-angle constraint memo coupling local inflation and macro lift for an orbital-ring tube. Key result: for slug lanes on a ring, local inflation scales with N_p m_dot u tan^2(alpha)/(π a^2), while net lift scales with 2 N_p m_dot (u/R - g_h/u). The axial speed component u must exceed orbital speed for positive lift.
